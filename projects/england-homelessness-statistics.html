APPLIED STATISTICS · MSc PROJECT 13

Statutory Homelessness in England — Statistical Trends

Exploring 29 quarters of statutory homelessness duties in England, with reproducible statistics and openly attributed aggregate data.

View Python source ↗Technical README ↗

Project context

Our MSc group investigated trends in statutory homelessness support duties following the Homelessness Reduction Act (2018), using government statistics and SAS. The public repository contains a focused 29-quarter data extract and a new Python workflow reproducing descriptive findings.

Methods

  • Validated and summarised available quarterly counts for initial assessments, prevention and relief duties.
  • Compared yearly means and examined variation across periods using one-way ANOVA.
  • Calculated the Pearson association between prevention and relief duty counts.
  • Fitted a simple time-trend regression and generated yearly summary and scatter charts.

Findings and interpretation

Using the supplied quarterly data, prevention and relief duties have a weak positive Pearson correlation (r ≈ 0.232). The simple total-duty regression has a slope of about +2,632 per year and R² ≈ 0.652. These are descriptive comparisons, not a causal estimate of legislative effects.

Research and reproducibility note
This was a group project, and work is not represented as exclusively mine. Government figures are an archived 2025 coursework snapshot. The observation periods differ by year, and trends in recorded duties do not necessarily measure all people experiencing homelessness. Official statistical data are attributed under OGL v3.0.
